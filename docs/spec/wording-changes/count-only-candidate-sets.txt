- - -> **The count over candidate sets.**
- - -> The receiver's engine forms round `k`'s pairs as step 1 of [Computing the count](#computing-the-count) does, the pair for a response position `q` matching setup position `p` being (`floor(q / w_R)`, `T_k[p]`).

- - -> The replay then takes this form, of which steps 2 and 3 of that section are the case where every round's pairs are disjoint:
- - -> Two sets start empty: the slots and the pseudonyms that have left candidacy.
- - -> Round by round, in the round order, the round's graph is its candidate pairs whose slot and pseudonym are both still in candidacy.
- - -> The round adds to the count the size of a maximum matching of that graph: the largest number of its pairs no two of which share a slot or a pseudonym.
- - -> Every slot and every pseudonym in a pair of the round's graph then leaves candidacy, whether or not a maximum matching includes the pair, as every record in a candidate pair does under `psi` ([Removal on a potential match](#removal-on-a-potential-match)).
- - -> The count is the sum of what the rounds add.
- - -> Only the size of a maximum matching enters the count, never which pairs it holds, and that size is a function of the graph alone.
- - -> The count therefore does not depend on the order of a record's candidates, the order the pairs are met in, `pi`, `sigma`, or which party is the receiver, and it is the same in every run on the same data.
- - -> Where every round's pairs are disjoint the graph is itself a matching, every pair counts, and the count is the one above.
- - -> **The double-match case.**
- - -> In [the normative double-match case](#the-normative-double-match-case) the round's candidate pairs are `(S0, R0)`, `(S0, R1)` and `(S1, R1)`, held as slots and pseudonyms.
- - -> A maximum matching has two pairs, `(S0, R0)` and `(S1, R1)`, so the round adds 2, the size of the table both `psi` parties produce.
- - -> A sweep meeting `(S0, R1)` first would accept one pair, and nothing in the slots and pseudonyms fixes which pair a sweep meets first.
- - -> **Comparability with `psi` over candidate sets.**
- - -> The `psi` sweep accepts a round's candidate pairs greedily in ascending (sender row, receiver row) ([Record-level resolution](#record-level-resolution-canonical-order-and-tiebreak)), so its table is a matching no further pair can join.
- - -> A maximum matching is at least as large and at most twice as large:
- - -> The two are equal wherever the sweep's table is a maximum matching, the double-match case included.
- - -> They differ where a pair the sweep accepts first blocks two later ones.
- - -> `S0` realizing `{ab, cd}` and `S1` realizing `{ef}`, against `R0` realizing `{ab, ef}` and `R1` realizing `{cd}`, give the pairs `(S0, R0)`, `(S0, R1)` and `(S1, R0)`.

- - -> Where no key value is repeated within either party's dataset, the count is therefore at least the size of the `psi` table and at most twice it, and each round adds the most pairs the `psi` sweep accepts on that round's pairs under any order of the two parties' rows.
