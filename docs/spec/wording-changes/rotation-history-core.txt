- - -> The previous secret and the rotation history
- - -> The key file schema admits three more optional fields, for the handshake's previous secret and rotation history ([PROTOCOL.md](PROTOCOL.md#the-previous-secret)):
- - -> **`previousSharedSecret`**: the secret a rotation this side stored as the initiator replaced, in the `sharedSecret` format, kept until the partner's first frame after the terms exchange shows it stored the same next secret.
- - -> **`previousExpires`**: the ISO 8601 `expires` that secret had as the current one.
- - -> **`rotationHistory`**: the history verifiers of the secrets the file retired, newest first, each an object holding `verifier`, 32 bytes as 43 base64url characters, and that secret's optional `expires`.
- - -> An entry holding any other field is refused.
- - -> A file whose field breaks its format is refused at load, as a malformed `expires` is.
- - -> No application writes or reads these fields for a run: the command line passes the handshake no previous secret and an empty history, and the web application's import of a command-line key file names `previousSharedSecret`, `previousExpires` and `rotationHistory` among the fields it drops.
- - -> The value that stands in for an absent previous secret is drawn the same way (`generateKey`).
- - -> `used_previous_byte` is `0x01` if the initiator completed under its previous secret and `0x00` otherwise (**Which secret a handshake uses**, below), so a changed flag fails the initiator's tag too.
- - -> `schedule_byte` names the message 2 field a responder tag is sent in: `0x00` for `confirm`, the tag under the responder's current secret, and `0x01` for `previousConfirm`, the tag under its second schedule.
- - -> So a tag moved to the other field does not verify.
- - -> `responder_confirm'` is the responder's tag under its second schedule, and `proof_R` and `proof_I` are history proofs ([The rotation history](#the-rotation-history)).
- - -> **Which secret a handshake uses.**
- - -> A party holds its current secret and, after a run cut before its partner stored the next secret, a previous one ([The previous secret](#the-previous-secret)).
- - -> Each side runs the key schedule twice on every handshake: under its current secret, and under its previous secret or, when it holds none, under a 32-byte value drawn for that handshake and then discarded.
- - -> So the work and message 2's shape do not depend on whether a previous secret is held.
- - -> **The responder** sends both schedules' responder tags in message 2, current first, as `confirm` and `previousConfirm`.
- - -> **The initiator** compares each of its schedules' expected responder tags against both received tags, the tag expected at each field made with that field's `schedule_byte`, all four comparisons made in constant time whatever matches.
- - -> It completes under the first match in the order: its current secret against `confirm`, then against `previousConfirm`, then its previous secret against `confirm`, then against `previousConfirm`.
- - -> Its drawn stand-in value is never chosen.
- - -> It sends `used_previous` true when it completed under its previous secret, bound under its tag.
- - -> **The responder** computes the expected initiator tag under both of its schedules with message 3's flag and wrapped value, and compares both whatever matches.
- - -> It completes under its current schedule where that tag verifies, and otherwise under its previous one, only where it holds a previous secret.
- - -> It unwraps `wrapped_N_I` under the session key of the schedule it completed under, and wraps `N_R` under the same one.
- - -> Each side so learns which secret each side used: the initiator from which of its schedules matched and in which field, which `schedule_byte` binds under the responder's tag, the responder from its own matching schedule and the flag.
- - -> A message 2 whose two tags are swapped in transit matches in neither field and fails with the generic error.
- - -> `authenticateConnection` returns both (`AuthResult.secretUsed`).
- - -> A tag under a secret the other side does not hold verifies nothing, so a party with neither secret learns nothing from either tag.
- - -> A previous secret whose `expires` has passed, or that equals the current secret, is not offered: that side runs the drawn value in its place.
- - -> **When no secret matches.**
- - -> Where no tag verifies on the initiator, it sends an abort holding its history proof, and a behind notice where the responder's proof matched its history.
- - -> A responder answers every abort holding a proof with an abort of its own, holding a behind notice where the initiator's proof matched its history; it does not answer an abort with no proof.
- - -> Each side then fails:
- - -> A side whose history matched the peer's proof at position `k` fails with `PartnerCopyBehindError` (`packages/core/src/errors.ts`), which states that the other side connected with a copy no more than `k` rotations behind and gives the confirm-with-your-partner and compromise steps.
- - -> The initiator sends its abort and fails at once.
- - -> Otherwise, a side that reads a behind notice that verifies under its own current secret's verifier fails with `LocalCopyBehindError`, which states that this copy is older than the other side's and does not repeat the count.
- - -> The initiator waits for the responder's reply within the per-message bound below; a reply that is missing, malformed or holds no notice that verifies leaves the generic failure.
- - -> Any other case is the generic failure.
- - -> Both errors are `AuthenticationError`s, so every consumer that classifies a failed key exchange classifies these the same way.
- - -> The two exceptions are the older-copy failures (**When no secret matches**, above), each shown only on a proof or notice that only a holder of this partnership's secrets can make.
- - -> Both schedules run on every handshake, the responder computes its history proof on every handshake, and each side checks a peer's proof against every history entry it checks, so the work does not depend on whether a previous secret is held or which entry matched.
- - -> The history check's work does grow with the number of entries checked, at most 64, which a peer can time; that count is at most the partnership's number of rotations, which the shared folder's operator or the coordination server can already observe from the run cadence.
- - -> **What a transport operator reads.**
- - -> On `sftp` and `filedrop` the server's or shared folder's operator reads the handshake frames in the clear: message 3's `usedPrevious`, and whether an abort holds a behind notice and its count.
- - -> So the operator sees when a run healed a cut rotation, or when a copy a few rotations old connected, metadata of the kind the run cadence it already sees gives.
- - -> They fix a previous secret and record, per combination, the schedule under it, the responder's tag with `schedule_byte` set and the initiator's tag with `used_previous_byte` set, and once both secrets' history verifiers, each role's history proof under the current secret's verifier, and each role's behind notice under the previous secret's verifier ([The rotation history](#the-rotation-history)).
- - -> An initiator that kept `X` as its previous secret heals that at the next run, whichever side initiates it ([The previous secret](#the-previous-secret)); neither application keeps one, so for both a re-invite recovers it.
- - -> The previous secret
- - -> `authenticateConnection` (`packages/core/src/auth.ts`) takes, beside the current secret, a `RotationState`: the previous secret with its own `expires`, when the side holds one, and the rotation history ([The rotation history](#the-rotation-history)).
- - -> It offers the previous secret to the handshake as **Which secret a handshake uses** ([P-256 authenticated key exchange](#p-256-authenticated-key-exchange)) states, and the `expires` it hands the terms exchange's checks is that of the secret the handshake used.
- - -> **Which store keeps it.**
- - -> The initiator's store keeps the secret its handshake used as the previous secret, beside the next one; the responder's store keeps none, since it is made only after `proceed` has shown that the initiator stored.
- - -> So a side holds a previous secret only after a run in which it stored as the initiator, and it may take either role in the next run.
- - -> **The drop.**
- - -> The initiator drops its previous secret when the responder's first frame after terms message 3 that is not an abort arrives: `runExchange` awaits the caller's `TermsRotation.partnerStored` once, before that frame is read, on the initiator only.
- - -> The responder stores before it sends any frame after `proceed`, so that frame shows the responder stored the next secret; it shows that only where the AEAD layer opened it.
- - -> A responder whose store failed sends an abort, which is not such a frame, so the initiator keeps its previous secret.
- - -> A drop that throws ends the run, after the initiator sends the responder an abort stating `"the partner could not save its copy of the shared secret"`, so the responder reports that its partner ended the run.
- - -> A completed run in each exchange mode -- cascade, single-pass and count-only -- sends such a frame, which `packages/core/test/initiatorDropsPreviousSecret.test.ts` checks in both PSI roles.
- - -> **Its life is one run.**
- - -> The next run in which that side stores replaces it: as initiator it keeps the secret that run used instead, and as responder it keeps none.
- - -> A previous secret whose `expires` has passed is not offered, and one equal to the current secret is not offered either.
- - -> The heal run rotates as any run does.
- - -> The initiator of the cut run is the side that may be ahead, and only its responder can be behind: before the initiator's store the responder cannot have stored, and after it the initiator holds the newer secret.
- - -> Neither application keeps a previous secret or a history: the command line (`apps/cli/src/protocol.ts`) and the browser (`apps/web/src/psi/authenticateExchange.ts`) pass no previous secret and an empty history, so a cut run is recovered by re-invite and a stale copy gets the generic failure.
- - -> The rotation history
- - -> The history is a list of **history verifiers**, one per secret a side retired, newest first, each with that secret's own `expires`.
- - -> A verifier is derived with the application HKDF from the 32-byte secret:
- - -> verifier = HKDF(secret, "alcove-rotation-history-v2:verifier", 32)
- - -> proof_<role> = HMAC(HKDF(verifier || dh, "alcove-rotation-history-v2:proof-<role>", 32), e_I || e_R)
- - -> notice_<role> = HMAC(HKDF(verifier || dh, "alcove-rotation-history-v2:notice-<role>", 32), u32be(k) || e_I || e_R)
- - -> `<role>` is the sender's handshake role, `initiator` or `responder`; `dh` is the handshake's `ee` output, the same on both sides whatever secrets they hold; `e_I` and `e_R` are the two ephemeral shares as sent; and `u32be(k)` is the notice's count as a 4-byte big-endian unsigned integer.
- - -> **The proof.**
- - -> A side makes its proof under the verifier of its own current secret: the responder in every message 2, the initiator in its abort when no tag verifies.
- - -> The shares are fresh in every handshake, so a proof cannot be replayed into another one.
- - -> **The check.**
- - -> A side checks a peer's proof against the 64 newest entries of its history, every entry whatever matches.
- - -> An entry whose `expires` has passed is not checked but keeps its place, so position `k`, counted from 1 at the newest, is always `k` rotations back.
- - -> A match at `k` shows that the peer's copy is no older than the secret this side retired `k` rotations ago.
- - -> **The notice.**
- - -> On a match at `k` a side sends a notice stating `k`, under the matched verifier, which is the verifier of the peer's current secret; the peer checks it under its own.
- - -> The distinct labels and the count inside the MAC input keep a proof, made under the same verifier, from verifying as a notice when a peer reflects it.
- - -> A notice that verifies shows only that the sender holds a copy newer than the receiver's; the count is the sender's statement, and `LocalCopyBehindError` does not show it.
- - -> The schema bounds the count to 1 through `2^32 - 1`.
- - -> **What a verifier grants.**
- - -> No handshake, peer id, relay key or AEAD key derives from a verifier, so a holder of the history can authenticate nothing it could not already.
- - -> A proof or notice is keyed from a verifier and `dh`, which only a holder of one of the handshake's ephemeral private keys computes, so a recorded handshake cannot be checked against a secret, or against a later copy of the history, after the fact.
- - -> The key file's `rotationHistory` field holds the list ([EXCHANGE_FILE.md](EXCHANGE_FILE.md#the-previous-secret-and-the-rotation-history)), and `deriveHistoryVerifier` in `@alcove/core` derives the entry for a secret being retired.
- - -> It is minted before the key exchange, so it is the current secret's whichever secret the handshake then completes under ([Which secret a handshake uses](#p-256-authenticated-key-exchange)), the key a party's own registrar holds once its registration of that secret has landed.
